CHASSIS STABILITY vs INSTANTANEOUS SLAM ERROR
================================================================================================
Pearson r between windowed chassis agitation and windowed
SLAM error, with a 95% percentile-bootstrap CI (2000 resamples).

Predictors, inside a 100 ms window:
  pitch_std / roll_std / agitation  std of attitude [rad]
  vibration_rms                     RMS |gt_a| [m/s^2], la aceleracion propia
                                    de verdad-terreno, NO el IMU: ver metrics_io

Responses, over the same window:
  ate_mean    mean instantaneous ATE [m].  ACCUMULATED error, so
              it carries the whole run up to that window; it
              correlates with WHERE on the route the window is
              as much as with what happened during it.
  ate_growth  ATE gained across the window [m].  This is the one
              that asks whether agitation DURING this window cost
              accuracy, and the one to quote.

A CI that spans 0 means this run cannot distinguish the effect
from none.  Read the sign only when it does not.

r|vel is the PARTIAL correlation with the windowed speed
discounted from both sides, r(predictor, response | speed).
It exists because agitation and per-metre error share an
obvious common cause: driving faster shakes the chassis AND
leaves fewer sweeps per metre, so a raw r can be the speed
looking at itself.  r|vel close to r means speed was not the
explanation; r|vel collapsing towards 0 means it was.
